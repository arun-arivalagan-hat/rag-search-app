# The Science of Sleep

Adults generally need seven to nine hours of sleep per night. Sleep is not a passive state: the brain cycles through distinct stages roughly every 90 minutes, and each stage serves a different purpose.

## Sleep stages

Sleep begins with light non-REM stages, then moves into slow-wave (deep) sleep, when the body repairs tissue and growth hormone is released. Deep sleep dominates the first half of the night. Rapid eye movement (REM) sleep, when most vivid dreaming occurs, becomes longer toward morning. REM is associated with memory consolidation and emotional processing.

## The body clock

Sleep timing is governed by the circadian rhythm, a roughly 24-hour internal clock coordinated by the suprachiasmatic nucleus in the hypothalamus. Morning light suppresses the hormone melatonin and tells the brain it is daytime. Bright screens in the evening can delay melatonin release and push bedtime later.

## Consequences of too little sleep

Chronic sleep deprivation impairs attention, slows reaction time and weakens the immune response. It is linked to higher risks of obesity, type 2 diabetes and hypertension. Practical advice includes keeping a consistent schedule, limiting caffeine after midday, keeping the bedroom cool and dark, and avoiding screens in the hour before bed.
