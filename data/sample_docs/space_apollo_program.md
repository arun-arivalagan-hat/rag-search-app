# The Apollo Program

Apollo was the NASA program that landed the first humans on the Moon. It was announced by President John F. Kennedy in 1961, when he committed the United States to landing a man on the Moon and returning him safely to Earth before the end of the decade.

## Key missions

Apollo 8, in December 1968, was the first crewed mission to orbit the Moon. Apollo 11 launched on 16 July 1969 atop a Saturn V rocket. On 20 July, Neil Armstrong and Buzz Aldrin landed the lunar module Eagle in the Sea of Tranquility while Michael Collins remained in lunar orbit aboard the command module Columbia. Armstrong stepped onto the surface first and the two astronauts spent about two and a half hours outside.

Apollo 13 in April 1970 suffered an oxygen tank explosion on the way to the Moon. The crew used the lunar module as a lifeboat and returned safely to Earth. In total, six Apollo missions landed on the Moon, ending with Apollo 17 in December 1972, and twelve astronauts walked on its surface.

## Legacy

The Saturn V remains the most powerful rocket to have flown successfully, generating about 7.6 million pounds of thrust at liftoff. The crews brought back 382 kilograms of lunar rock and soil, which scientists still study today. The program also drove advances in integrated circuits and software engineering, including the guidance computer that flew the lunar module.
