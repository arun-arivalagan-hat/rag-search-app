# Exploring Mars

Mars is the fourth planet from the Sun and the most explored planet beyond Earth. Its thin atmosphere is about 95 percent carbon dioxide, and surface pressure is less than one percent of Earth's. Average temperatures hover near minus 60 degrees Celsius, though equatorial summer days can briefly reach above 20 degrees.

## Rovers

NASA's Sojourner landed in 1997 as part of the Pathfinder mission and was the first wheeled vehicle on Mars. The twin rovers Spirit and Opportunity arrived in 2004. Opportunity was designed for a 90-day mission but kept operating for nearly fifteen years. Curiosity landed in Gale Crater in 2012 using a rocket-powered sky crane, and found evidence that the crater once held a freshwater lake with the chemical ingredients needed for microbial life.

The Perseverance rover touched down in Jezero Crater in February 2021. Jezero was chosen because it contains a well-preserved river delta. Perseverance collects rock core samples and seals them in tubes for a future return mission. It also carried the Ingenuity helicopter, which made the first powered, controlled flight on another planet.

## Why water matters

Orbital images show dry river valleys and minerals that form only in the presence of liquid water. Scientists believe Mars was warmer and wetter more than three billion years ago. Understanding where that water went, and whether life ever arose, is the central question driving Martian exploration.
