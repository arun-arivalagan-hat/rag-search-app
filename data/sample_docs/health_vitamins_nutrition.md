# Vitamins and Nutrition Basics

Vitamins are organic compounds the body needs in small amounts but cannot make in sufficient quantity, so they must come from food. There are thirteen essential vitamins, split into fat-soluble (A, D, E and K) and water-soluble (C and the eight B vitamins).

## Fat-soluble versus water-soluble

Fat-soluble vitamins are absorbed with dietary fat and stored in the liver and fatty tissue, so excess intake over a long period can build up to harmful levels. Water-soluble vitamins are not stored in large amounts; surplus is excreted in urine, so they need regular replenishment.

## Selected vitamins

- **Vitamin C** supports collagen synthesis and iron absorption. Severe deficiency causes scurvy, historically common among sailors on long voyages without fresh fruit.
- **Vitamin D** helps the body absorb calcium and is produced in the skin on exposure to sunlight. Low levels are linked to weak bones, such as rickets in children.
- **Vitamin B12** is needed for nerve function and red blood cell formation. It occurs naturally almost only in animal products, so vegans typically need a supplement.
- **Vitamin K** is required for blood clotting.

## Getting enough

A varied diet rich in vegetables, fruit, whole grains, legumes and lean protein covers most needs for most people. Supplements help in specific cases such as pregnancy, restricted diets or diagnosed deficiency, but they do not replace a balanced diet.
