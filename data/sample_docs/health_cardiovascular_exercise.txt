Exercise and Heart Health

Regular aerobic exercise strengthens the heart muscle, lowers resting heart rate and improves how efficiently the body delivers oxygen. Health guidelines from the World Health Organization recommend at least 150 minutes of moderate-intensity activity, or 75 minutes of vigorous activity, each week for adults.

Moderate activity includes brisk walking, cycling on level ground and water aerobics. Vigorous activity includes running, swimming laps and fast cycling. A useful rule of thumb for moderate effort is the talk test: you can talk but not sing. Maximum heart rate is often estimated as 220 minus age, and moderate exercise typically falls between 50 and 70 percent of that figure.

Exercise lowers blood pressure, raises HDL ("good") cholesterol and reduces triglycerides. It also improves insulin sensitivity, which helps control blood sugar. People who are physically active have a substantially lower risk of coronary artery disease, stroke and heart failure.

Strength training twice a week complements aerobic work by building muscle, which helps regulate glucose and supports bone density. Beginners should increase duration and intensity gradually, and anyone with existing heart conditions should consult a doctor before starting a new programme. Rest days matter too, because the heart and muscles adapt during recovery rather than during the workout itself.
